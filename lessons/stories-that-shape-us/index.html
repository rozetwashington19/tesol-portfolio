THE INSTRUCTIONAL CHALLENGE

How can students develop rich ideas without having to generate a story and solve every English-language demand at once?

01 / CONTEXT

The learners.

Grade 7 students begin a personal narrative unit. They can choose an object, place, or experience, or invent a character and event. Personal disclosure is always optional.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Content and language,
planned together.

CONTENT OBJECTIVE

Analyze how a narrative communicates identity and develop a short narrative about something meaningful.

LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE

Use sequencing words, sensory details, and a statement of significance to tell a short story.

Framework connection

WIDA Standard 2: Language for Language Arts · Key Language Use: Narrate. This is a broad planning connection; grade-specific language expectations and NYS content-standard mapping should be finalized for the teaching context.

Explore the WIDA framework

03 / LANGUAGE SUPPORTS

Two languages.
More ways in.

Pair English and Spanish prompts. Invite home-language thinking and oral rehearsal, then support English production. Adapt the home-language supports to the learners in the room.

ENGLISHESPAÑOL

English___ is important to me because ___.

Español___ es importante para mí porque ___.

EnglishWhen ___ happened, I felt ___.

EspañolCuando pasó ___, me sentí ___.

Adjust support through images, dictation, partner talk, vocabulary banks, and gradual removal of frames. Proficiency labels describe the intended learner profile, not fixed limits on thinking.

04 / LESSON SEQUENCE

A purposeful progression.

01

12 min · Connect

Explore identity objects and brainstorm an identity web in any language.

02

10 min · Interpret

Read The Old Pot / La olla vieja and mark details, emotions, and sequence.

03

06 min · Model

Think aloud through the beginning, middle, and significance of a sketchbook narrative.

04

08 min · Rehearse

Tell a partner the story; the listener asks a clarifying question.

05

24 min · Draft and reflect

Write a supported paragraph, review the checklist, and reflect on helpful strategies.

05 / STUDENT ACTIVITIES

Make the thinking visible.

An identity web becomes a beginning–middle–end plan, then a short narrative. Include at least one sensory detail and an explanation of why the experience matters.

06 / ASSESSMENT

Notice what students know.

Look for focus, significance, description, sequence, and clear communication. Respond with one strength and one actionable revision step. Assess ideas separately from scaffold use.

Separate content understanding from the amount of English-language support needed to communicate it.

07 / REFLECTION

Why this design.

Bilingual planning values students’ existing resources. Oral rehearsal makes it easier to organize a story before writing, while a fictional option protects privacy and choice.

This is a planned instructional design. After teaching, collect anonymized evidence, compare initial and final responses, and identify the next support to keep, change, or remove.
