THE INSTRUCTIONAL CHALLENGE

How can students distinguish an opinion from an argument and explain why evidence supports a claim?

01 / CONTEXT

The learners.

Grade 10 learners consider how a hypothetical school phone policy could balance learning and student needs. No particular viewpoint is required.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Content and language,
planned together.

CONTENT OBJECTIVE

Evaluate multiple perspectives and construct a defensible, evidence-based claim.

LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE

State a claim, attribute evidence, explain reasoning, and respond to a counterclaim with academic connectors.

Framework connection

WIDA Standard 2: Language for Language Arts · Key Language Use: Argue. This is a broad planning connection; grade-specific language expectations and NYS content-standard mapping should be finalized for the teaching context.

Explore the WIDA framework

03 / LANGUAGE SUPPORTS

Two languages.
More ways in.

Pair English and Spanish prompts. Invite home-language thinking and oral rehearsal, then support English production. Adapt the home-language supports to the learners in the room.

ENGLISHESPAÑOL

EnglishAccording to ___, ___. This supports my claim because ___.

EspañolSegún ___, ___. Esto respalda mi postura porque ___.

EnglishSome people argue ___. However, ___.

EspañolAlgunas personas sostienen que ___. Sin embargo, ___.

Adjust support through images, dictation, partner talk, vocabulary banks, and gradual removal of frames. Proficiency labels describe the intended learner profile, not fixed limits on thinking.

04 / LESSON SEQUENCE

A purposeful progression.

01

06 min · Take a position

Choose an initial position on an opinion continuum, with a private response option.

02

10 min · Model reasoning

Separate claim, evidence, and reasoning in a verified passage.

03

18 min · Evaluate sources

Compare a research source, student perspective, and policy example.

04

14 min · Discuss

Use roles and sentence stems to question, add evidence, and respond.

05

32 min · Argue and reflect

Draft an argument and explain whether evidence changed the initial position.

05 / STUDENT ACTIVITIES

Make the thinking visible.

A short argument includes a claim, accurate attributed evidence, reasoning, a fair counterclaim, and a rebuttal. Students can plan bilingually before producing an English draft.

06 / ASSESSMENT

Notice what students know.

Score claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, and language. Evaluate the quality of reasoning rather than agreement with the teacher. Record supports separately from task performance.

Separate content understanding from the amount of English-language support needed to communicate it.

07 / REFLECTION

Why this design.

The progression from opinion to evidence and discussion gives students a purpose for academic language. The challenge remains rigorous while the language becomes more accessible.

This is a planned instructional design. After teaching, collect anonymized evidence, compare initial and final responses, and identify the next support to keep, change, or remove.
