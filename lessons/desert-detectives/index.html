Desert Detectives

Observe. Describe. Explain.

THE INSTRUCTIONAL CHALLENGE

How can students explain a scientific relationship while developing the language needed to describe it?

01 / CONTEXT

The learners.

Grade 2 learners explore habitats and animal survival. Photographs, partner talk, gestures, and bilingual labels create multiple entry points to the same science concept.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Content and language,
planned together.

CONTENT OBJECTIVE

Identify two desert characteristics and explain one way a desert animal survives.

LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE

Describe an animal and connect a survival feature to its function using a supported oral or written explanation.

Framework connection

WIDA Standard 4: Language for Science · Key Language Use: Explain. This is a broad planning connection; grade-specific language expectations and NYS content-standard mapping should be finalized for the teaching context.

Explore the WIDA framework

03 / LANGUAGE SUPPORTS

Two languages.
More ways in.

Pair English and Spanish prompts. Invite home-language thinking and oral rehearsal, then support English production. Adapt the home-language supports to the learners in the room.

ENGLISHESPAÑOL

EnglishThe ___ has ___, which helps it ___.

EspañolEl/La ___ tiene ___, lo que le ayuda a ___.

EnglishBecause the desert is ___, the animal ___.

EspañolComo el desierto es ___, el animal ___.

Adjust support through images, dictation, partner talk, vocabulary banks, and gradual removal of frames. Proficiency labels describe the intended learner profile, not fixed limits on thinking.

04 / LESSON SEQUENCE

A purposeful progression.

01

08 min · Notice

Use See–Think–Wonder with a desert photograph. Rehearse observations with a partner.

02

08 min · Build vocabulary

Match images and English–Spanish labels; say, gesture, and use each word.

03

14 min · Model together

Connect a camel’s fat-storing hump and a fennec fox’s large ears to their functions.

04

12 min · Investigate

Read an animal card together and complete an organizer.

05

18 min · Create and reflect

Make a survival poster, explain it, and complete an exit ticket.

05 / STUDENT ACTIVITIES

Make the thinking visible.

A mini survival poster includes an animal, a desert characteristic, an adaptation, and an explanatory sentence. Students may draw before writing and rehearse in either language.

06 / ASSESSMENT

Notice what students know.

Check scientific accuracy and whether the explanation links a feature to survival. Record the amount of language support separately. Accept a written response, a drawing with oral explanation, or a recording.

Separate content understanding from the amount of English-language support needed to communicate it.

07 / REFLECTION

Why this design.

Observation lowers the language barrier at the start. Modeling reveals the reasoning, and partner rehearsal helps students move toward an independent explanation.

This is a planned instructional design. After teaching, collect anonymized evidence, compare initial and final responses, and identify the next support to keep, change, or remove.
