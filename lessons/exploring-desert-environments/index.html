How can beginning multilingual learners share observations before they can produce extended English?

01 / CONTEXT

The learners.

A hypothetical K–1 class explores a hot desert habitat. This is a sample unit concept, distinct from the completed Grade 2 lesson.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Content and language,
planned together.

CONTENT OBJECTIVE

Notice features of a desert and match a pictured animal to its habitat.

LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE

Name a pictured feature and use “I see ___” or “The desert is ___” with visuals or partner support.

Framework connection

WIDA Standard 4: Language for Science · Key Language Use: Inform + Explain. This is a broad planning connection; grade-specific language expectations and NYS content-standard mapping should be finalized for the teaching context.

Explore the WIDA framework

03 / LANGUAGE SUPPORTS

Two languages.
More ways in.

Pair English and Spanish prompts. Invite home-language thinking and oral rehearsal, then support English production. Adapt the home-language supports to the learners in the room.

ENGLISHESPAÑOL

EnglishI see ___.

EspañolVeo ___.

EnglishThe desert is dry.

EspañolEl desierto es seco.

Adjust support through images, dictation, partner talk, vocabulary banks, and gradual removal of frames. Proficiency labels describe the intended learner profile, not fixed limits on thinking.

04 / LESSON SEQUENCE

A purposeful progression.

01

Session 1 · Notice

Use See–Think–Wonder and model picture-supported observations.

02

Session 2 · Name and connect

Practice desert/desierto, water/agua, and shade/sombra; sort animal and habitat cards.

03

Session 3 · Create

Work together on a habitat poster with bilingual labels and one oral explanation each.

05 / STUDENT ACTIVITIES

Make the thinking visible.

A collaborative poster combines drawings, labeled picture cards, and student dictation. Each learner contributes through pointing, naming, drawing, or a supported sentence.

06 / ASSESSMENT

Notice what students know.

Use a child-friendly “I can notice / I can name / I can explain” check. Observe the understanding behind a point, drawing, or utterance before deciding what support comes next.

Separate content understanding from the amount of English-language support needed to communicate it.

07 / REFLECTION

Why this design.

Short repeated routines give young learners familiarity. Images and home-language connections support access while students build confidence participating in English.

This is a planned instructional design. After teaching, collect anonymized evidence, compare initial and final responses, and identify the next support to keep, change, or remove.
