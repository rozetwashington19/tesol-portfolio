04 / MY TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

“Language learning should expand a student’s voice, not replace it.”

ROZARIO WASHINGTON · PERSONAL TEACHING STATEMENT

I see multilingualism as an asset. Students arrive with languages, identities, experiences, and community knowledge that deserve a place in the classroom. I want those resources to become a foundation for learning.

Access begins with intentional instruction. I use visuals, modeling, clear language, and strategic pacing to make input understandable. Sentence frames, graphic organizers, and differentiated tasks offer support without reducing the thinking students are asked to do.

Language grows through meaningful use. Collaborative discussion, real-world questions, and connections to students’ lives create reasons to read, write, listen, and speak. Home-language planning and translanguaging help students develop ideas before expressing them in new ways.

A safe classroom gives students room to take risks and be heard. I check understanding frequently, listen to student voice, and adjust instruction. As confidence and independence grow, I gradually release responsibility and remove supports students no longer need.

TEACHING IN PRACTICE

How I support multilingual learners.

Purposeful supports. Meaningful participation. A gradual path toward independence.

01

Comprehensible Input

Visuals, modeling, gestures, chunking, and strategic pacing.

Show a photograph, model a short explanation, and check understanding before adding new vocabulary.
02

Scaffolding

Sentence frames, word banks, graphic organizers, and exemplars.

Move from a completed model to a shared response, then invite students to explain independently.
03

Translanguaging

The complete linguistic repertoire as a resource for learning.

Invite home-language brainstorming and bilingual labels, followed by supported English rehearsal.
04

Academic Language

Vocabulary, language functions, structured talk, reading, and writing.

Teach the language of explaining, narrating, and arguing alongside the content of a lesson.
05

Culturally Responsive Teaching

Students’ identities and lived experiences as a starting point.

Offer meaningful choices and connections without asking students to represent an entire culture.
06

Assessment

Observation, conferencing, student work, and frequent checks.

Separate conceptual understanding from English proficiency and use evidence to plan the next step.
